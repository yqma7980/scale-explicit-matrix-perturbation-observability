\section{Response-route theorem validation}
For the response equation
\[
  A\delta z=-Ez,\qquad A=J+E,
\]
the computed route uses
\[
  \widetilde f=\operatorname{fl}(-E\widetilde z),\qquad
  r_\delta=\widetilde f-A\widetilde{\delta z},\qquad
  q_f=\widetilde f+E\widetilde z.
\]
With majorants
$\beta_A\geq\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_2$,
$\alpha_E\geq\lVert E\rVert_2$, and
$\lVert\widetilde z-z\rVert_2\leq U_z$, the implemented bound is
\[
 U_{\rm r}
 =
 \beta_A\alpha_EU_z
 +\beta_A\lVert q_f\rVert_2
 +\beta_A\lVert r_\delta\rVert_2.
\]
The development design was reused only after this route model was fixed. A new
300-system holdout then used disjoint seeds and registered condition,
dimension, perturbation-amplitude, and family grids.

\section{Precision and implementation independence}
The registered high-precision implementation was repeated at 120 and 180
decimal digits. The maximum normalized difference was
$5.16\times10^{-110}$ on development and $6.82\times10^{-107}$ on the new
holdout, with no coverage-decision change. A separately implemented
Python \texttt{Decimal} backend evaluated 36 stratified holdout systems in
both reference contracts. Across 72 lane comparisons, the maximum normalized
difference from the registered reference was $6.96\times10^{-153}$.

The first G2-20 execution stopped because its precision comparator normalized
near-zero quantities by their own magnitude. That attempt remains classified
as an inconclusive reference-or-precision result. Two tooling-only corrections
replaced the comparator without changing operators, thresholds, response
formulas, or scientific outputs; the corrected run restarted from row 1. This
lineage is reported rather than overwritten.

\section{Effectivity anatomy and extreme ratios}
For positive reference error $e_{\rm ref}$ and response magnitude
$d_{\rm ref}$, define
\[
 I_{\rm eff}=\frac{U}{e_{\rm ref}},
 \qquad
 R_U=\frac{U}{d_{\rm ref}}.
\]
For a parent bound $U=\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_F\lVert r\rVert_2$ and exact
forward error $e=\lVert A^{-1}r\rVert_2$,
\[
 I_{\rm eff}
 =
 \underbrace{\frac{\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_F}
 {\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_2}}_{\text{norm-majorant inflation}}
 \underbrace{\frac{\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_2\lVert r\rVert_2}
 {\lVert A^{-1}r\rVert_2}}_{\text{directional inflation}}.
\]

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\footnotesize
\caption{Factorized direct-route effectivity anatomy}\label{tab:S5}
\input{T3_effectivity_anatomy_v0.13.0.tex}
\end{table}

The norm-majorant median was approximately $1.0001$ on development and
$1.0050$ on the original holdout. Directional-inflation medians instead
ranged from $1.36\times10^3$ to $2.31\times10^4$. Thus the central
conservativeness is associated primarily with replacing the realized residual
direction by a worst-direction inverse norm.

The largest finite direct-route effectivity was
$7.9321\times10^{86}$, reported in the manuscript as
$\log_{10}I_{\rm eff}=86.90$. It occurred for
\texttt{HOLDOUT\_SPD\_D16\_C01\_E3eM17}, whose reference error was
approximately $4.9995\times10^{-102}$. The extreme ratio is therefore a
near-zero-denominator event, not undercoverage. Exact strings and case
identifiers remain in \texttt{Paper2\_extreme\_ratio\_record.json}.

A registered main-effects analysis gave adjusted $R^2=0.869$ on development;
the drop-one partial $R^2$ for condition exponent was $0.855$. The original
holdout fit gave adjusted $R^2=0.057$ and condition partial $R^2=0.029$.
These finite-grid results support mechanism analysis within the registered
design, not a universal conditioning law.
